> # Kenshi — A Review
> 
> **Developer:** Lo-Fi Games | **Genre:** Open-World Sandbox RPG | **Platform:** PC
> 
> ---
> 
> There is a moment early in Kenshi — maybe your third or fourth hour in — where you realize the game is never going to hold your hand. Your character has just been beaten unconscious by a wandering band of bandits, left bleeding in the dirt of a sun-scorched wasteland, with no quest marker telling you where to go and no tutorial pop-up offering consolation. And somehow, impossibly, you can't wait to get back up.
> 
> Kenshi is not a game of questlines. It's a game of emergent stories — of being a shopkeeper and a thief, a lone wanderer and a slave, and eventually an entire community of people working together to one day erect a city in the wasteland. The game never scripted any of it. It just gave you a world and stepped back.
> 
> At its core, Kenshi is an open-world RPG with real-time strategy elements and no linear narrative, set in a post-apocalyptic world where survival is brutally difficult. You begin with no skills and struggle simply to stay alive, leveling up abilities through use — thievery by stealing, combat by fighting — and slowly recruiting others to grow your band.
> 
> Eventually it becomes a surprisingly effective management game as you research technologies, construct buildings, and assign your people long lists of automated tasks — mining, farming, construction. The scope of what you can build toward is staggering. The path to get there is unforgiving.
> 
> That difficulty is the game's defining trait and its biggest barrier. The concept of a free-roaming RPG where you craft your own story is always intriguing, and the world is vast and impressive for a small indie project, but the game does a poor job of communicating its intricate systems to new players. There is no onboarding. You are expected to fail, learn, and try again. Whether that sounds like a feature or a flaw will determine whether Kenshi is for you.
> 
> At first glance it looks like a psychotic, twisted experiment. The graphics engine barely holds itself together under its many systems. There are bugs, rough optimization, and slow loading times. Visually, it will never win awards. The mechanics, however, are tremendous and extremely detailed — clearly a labor of love deserving of high praise.
> 
> What makes Kenshi genuinely special is its atmosphere. The world feels ancient, indifferent, and alive in ways most games can't replicate. Factions have their own ideologies and agendas. Cities feel lived-in. Every biome — from scorched flats to swamp-laden coastlines — carries a distinct personality. The world is fascinating, the myriad of ways to approach and play it enormous, and for those it grabs, it could easily consume all of your free time.
> 
> By May 2026, the game had sold 3 million copies — a remarkable achievement for a small studio with a niche, demanding product. Word of mouth and Steam reviews have kept it alive for years, and a dedicated modding community has only deepened the experience.
> 
> Kenshi is not a game for everyone. It is slow, obtuse, rough around every edge, and asks a great deal of patience before it gives anything back. But for those willing to meet it on its own terms, it offers something rare: a world that generates genuine legend. Your stories won't come from a writer. They'll come from the dust, the violence, and the unlikely triumphs you carved out yourself.
> 
> **Verdict: 8/10** — A singular, deeply flawed masterpiece of emergent storytelling. Brutal, beautiful, and completely unlike anything else.

> 
> I do however have 2 negatives to bring up. With the Sci-Fi setting not having any kind of firearms is a hugely missed opportunity and feels like they should be there, but it's missing. > 
> Some more explained details on the world, the in game mechanics and a more interactive tutorial wouldn't go amiss. But besides these 2 things I don't really have any complaints about it, except that I would personally like if the dialogue was voice acted. > 
> I was a bit sceptical at first since the gameplay looked more action oriented than it is, and the game doesn't have controller support. But a good way to describe the controls is Kotor, but with less combat options, base building and a larger party system.
> 
> Overall I give Kenshi 8.5/10. If guns (natively, not through mods), a longer and interactive tutorial, and voice acting are added it'll easily become a full 10/10!

### Citation 2 of 3

recommends, 66.6 hours at review, 2026-05-26

> I always struggle to review games like this, because the community is amazing and consistently releases new content and mods for this game. With that, I'll just be touching on the vanilla release, as with mods you can make it into whatever you want.
> 
> So Kenshi, what is it? It's a squad based open world game with a focus on community, allowing you to build your own structures, towns, and even empire. Unlike most other games of this nature, theres a huge focus on individual's rather than hero units or trainable soldiers. Your main team will be your main team for a majority of your sessions. I found it quite difficult to manage more than 3 squads at a time, 2 exploration teams and a 3rd to run and guard the base. This works well and allows you to limit the size of your operation to whatever you personally find managable.
> 
> Kenshi is a real time strategy, so you wont be influencing fights directly as much as youll be managing squad macro and keeping your people alive and healthy. The main hurdle when starting is learning that you're MEANT to get your ass kicked a few times. It's just counterintuitive and hard to learn. 
> 
> I have nothing but nice things to say about this janky buggy mess, I adore it and if you put the time in you might as well. New players would probably benifet from a tutorial start, but I'm not sure Kenshi even cares if you play it. Its a desolate world that would keep on scrapping by, with or without you. The ancient robots, ruins and sparse wastes hold several worlds worth of lore and story just waiting to be found. The lore is probably the best part of Kenshi, realizing this world is much older and much more complicated than you ever imagined. Strong 8/10 from me.
