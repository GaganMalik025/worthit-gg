# Titles whose cohort structure is live-service shaped: an open-ended
# progression loop that concentrates the pool in the veteran bucket, leaving
# the playtime split less to say than it has on a title people finish.
#
# WHY THIS LIST EXISTS RATHER THAN A PRICE CHECK
# ----------------------------------------------
# Free-to-play was the obvious automatic proxy - it costs nothing, the signal
# sits in store HTML we already cache. It was measured against 20 ingested
# titles on 2026-08-01 and it does not hold:
#
#     class                     n    veteran share (median)
#     free-to-play             10           50.4%
#     paid live-service         4           47.7%
#     paid, not live-service    6           32.9%
#
# Free and paid-live-service are indistinguishable. Rust (paid, 57.5% veteran)
# ranks above 8 of the 10 free titles; Team Fortress 2 (free, 36.5%) ranks below
# 3 of the 4 paid live-service ones. Price would have demoted TF2 and kept Rust,
# which is backwards. Being free does not make a game live-service; it is the
# progression loop that does, and no keyless field reports that.
#
# The refund-cohort half of the original worry was not supported at all. Free
# titles carry refund cohorts of 21-88 (median 49) against 12-54 (median 28) for
# the paid seeds. Cyberpunk 2077 - paid, single-player - owns the only cohort
# below invariant 12's floor of 20.
#
# WHAT THIS LIST IS NOT
# ---------------------
# Not an exclusion. Titles here are capped in the manifest, not dropped: people
# search them, and a verdict that says "the veterans are the game" is still a
# true and useful verdict. Helldivers 2 sits at 45.1% veteran - the highest of
# any paid seed - and produced the best verdict shape in the whole eval set.
# High veteran share compresses the segmentation story; it does not delete it.
#
# FORMAT: appid  # title - basis
#   "measured" = veteran share observed from a real ingest, cited inline
#   "judgment" = classified by its progression loop, not yet measured
#
